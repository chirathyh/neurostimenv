# Observed v4 qualification findings

These results come from a **CPU reanalysis of the existing 576 full-network episodes**, not a new prospective experiment. The data contain 256 training contexts on four circuits, 64 development contexts on two circuits, and 16 evaluation contexts on four circuits, with 16 futures per evaluation context. All four evaluation circuits have already been inspected. Results remain exploratory even though every new checkpoint, shrinkage factor, variance calibration and candidate selection used training/development data only.

The final qualification artifacts were produced at `/tmp/eegrepr-v4-completed-release-20261113/existing` during implementation. Numeric evidence lives under `models/`, `scores/`, `replication/`, `retention/`, `geometry/`, and `measurement/`; `qualification_key_findings.json` collects the prediction findings, while the retention and geometry summaries record their complete analyses. Each derived panel retains the imported generation provenance and its own new acquisition/target/normalization fingerprint. The original source archive SHA256 is `c88d205875ca9b63770c48dc3f71ce46be9861b5c98973bcda662c7c940589aa`.

## What ran

Six predeclared existing-data jobs crossed the three named inputs with two waveform targets: lead/duration 2/32 ms and 10/128 ms. Each job fitted 25 candidates: the complete 14-candidate ridge grid, two low-rank state models, two training-mean variance references, persistence, and six small neural candidates (MSE/Gaussian loss, three optimization seeds each). All numeric model states and train/development/evaluation predictions were saved. Evaluation losses were computed only by the separate score stage. No model or acquisition was selected using evaluation performance.

The neural engineering control used the **same small-network training path** and achieved 99.863% skill on a known deterministic held-out target, with 676 parameters. This qualifies the implementation path; it is not biophysical evidence. The actual four-example memorization slice contains **one circuit and four contexts**, and the new diagnostic records that count correctly. Checkpoints use development early stopping, and bounded residual shrinkage retains the training-mean fallback. Three variance diagnostics are now saved: uncalibrated, development-only multiplicative calibration, and `matched_calibrated`. The last is the primary comparison: an identically specified context-independent diagonal variance vector, fitted separately to each frozen mean on development data only. Multiplicative calibration gives identical calibration access but retains a Gaussian model's heteroscedasticity, so it does not by itself match final variance expressivity. Three optimization seeds are fitting repeats, not extra independent observations.

## H1: Prediction-risk accounting

The original physical targets and original risk reference reproduce: state-conditioned variance is 0.9792870366 standardized MSE; training-mean risk is 1.0213504738. Their ratio is 0.9588158637. The corresponding physical state variance is `1.3753135265e-19 V²`. These values refer to this exact waveform target and panel. They are **not the EEG-history Bayes floor**, a fraction of all EEG information, or separate estimates of observation, compression and fitting losses.

The old roughly 800k-parameter models remain preserved: their risks were approximately 1.31–1.44 times the mean baseline, with strong train/development overfitting. The new, much more conservative qualification pipeline substantially reduces that avoidable failure, but its evaluation improvements over the mean remain very small:

Fixing the acquisition definition or exposing native bins does not by itself solve this forecasting task.

In the preserved multiplicative-calibration diagnostic, the six candidates have nominal 95% marginal coverage of **92.65–93.94%**. Their development-calibrated Gaussian NLL is worse than the corresponding uncalibrated unit-variance mean baseline in every job. Calibration also slightly worsens NLL relative to the same selected model's uncalibrated variance in these six cases. Development calibration is an intervention whose outcome must be measured, not a guarantee of better held-out calibration. Detailed 50/80/95% coverage, widths, CRPS, PIT values and residual-coordinate dependence are saved. Marginal coverage does not establish joint temporal calibration.

For example, on original A0/10/128, uncalibrated/calibrated 95% coverage is 93.335%/93.903%, and marginal standardized NLL is 1.43739/1.44021 versus 1.42961 for the unit-variance mean. The selected physical MSE is `1.4330125084e-19 V²`, and its mean calibrated 95% interval width is `1.4835159821e-9 V`. Density artifacts distinguish the marginal Jacobian `log(y_scale)` from the joint diagonal Jacobian `dimension*log(y_scale)`.

## Uncertainty and the four-circuit limit

The selected A0 short-target model improves three of four circuits and loses 1.67% skill on circuit 35008. Its exploratory circuit-bootstrap skill interval is approximately **−0.906% to +1.379%**. A1 and A2 short-target results also include circuit reversals. The original A0 long-target ridge improves all four circuits, but only by 0.017–0.222% per circuit. Its positive four-circuit bootstrap interval is not evidence of a powered confirmation: with four paired circuit signs, the minimum two-sided exact sign-flip p-value is 0.125.

Future Monte Carlo precision is reported separately. Regimes share corresponding future streams, so the new calculation synchronously deletes the same future index across regimes within each circuit. On original A0/10/128, the conditional standard error of the state-variance estimate is 0.0347054 standardized MSE; the selected model's corrected excess standard error is 0.0105965. These are neither population confidence intervals nor standard errors of the model's skill contrast. Population summaries cluster by circuit; an additional hierarchical diagnostic resamples circuits, nested prefixes and paired future indices. Repeated futures and optimizer seeds do not increase independent circuit N.

## H2: Estimation with few independent futures

The new H2 stage ran nested M=1/2/4/8 analyses for all six qualified jobs. The source panel still contains **zero independent reference futures**, so reference-corrected estimation error and reference-aware intervals are explicitly unavailable in these data. They are implemented and tested using independent synthetic pools, including the correction `mean-distance² − reference-variance/L`, uncertain variance comparisons, and intervals that include uncertainty from both pools. Negative corrected estimates are retained.

The historical M=16 zero subsample spread remains a conditional reuse artifact: every full-size subset contains the same 16 futures. It does not imply zero future Monte Carlo uncertainty. The original single completed fixed-budget allocation, `(K=256, M=1)`, is not evidence for the unrun allocations. One future per training context still permits pooled variance learning under modeling assumptions; a poor fitted model does not establish impossibility.

## H3: Task-relevant compression

The final retention analysis completed the declared existing-data grid: **1,177 recorded rows, with 1,092 complete, 84 unavailable and one unsupported**. The 84 unavailable rows are eight-label carrier cases: eight labels cannot cover all three carrier classes in each of four training circuits, which requires at least 12 labels. The unsupported endpoint is feature18 at d32; its native width is 18 and padding is not treated as a representation. These rows are analysis endpoints, not independent experiments or extra circuit replicates. No frozen upstream neural encoder was retrained for this reanalysis.

The preserved matched d8/B32/linear/all-label carrier result is feature18 11/12 correct, PCA 4/12, untrained 6/12, masked 5/12, and forecast 2/12. Past reconstruction NMSE is approximately 0.98465, 0.93143, 0.97735, 0.94696 and 1.00219, respectively. This descriptive ordering illustrates that reconstruction and task accessibility need not agree. It compares complete encoder/projection/quantizer/decoder pipelines, not objective quality in isolation.

The new unquantized controls separate projection from subsequent quantization. Each table entry is **correct carrier labels out of 12 / decoder cross entropy in bits**, conditional on rhythmic membership. Linear logistic readout regularization was selected using equal circuit-weighted development loss, with all available training labels. Projection can improve a fitted decoder, as it does strongly for feature18 here, without increasing the underlying information. It changes regularization and which task distinctions are accessible to that decoder. Conversely, untrained and masked native-width readouts lose accuracy after projection. Quantization can also alter fitted-decoder behavior in either direction on this tiny panel. Neither cross entropy nor reconstruction error measures total mutual information. Raw-history identity reconstruction and deterministic PCA inverse reconstruction are separately saved; a poor raw-history task readout does not invalidate exact identity reconstruction.

The single frozen matched-code contrast is now actually scored: feature18 versus forecast, job `A0__T0_l10_d128`, native pooled input, d8/B32, training quantile range, linear readout, all labels, conditional carrier. Cross entropy is **0.187693 versus 1.593519 bits**. Forecast-minus-feature18 loss is positive on all four circuits: 1.357080, 1.505254, 1.221982 and 1.538986 bits for circuits 35006–35009, respectively. The equal circuit mean difference is **1.405825 bits**, exact two-sided circuit sign-flip **p = 0.125** (16 permutations, also the minimum attainable p), and the exploratory 95% percentile circuit-bootstrap interval is **[1.289531, 1.522120] bits**. The positive bootstrap interval does not remove the four-circuit limitation or establish confirmatory significance. Both historical banks have only one frozen optimization repeat. Prospective fitting repeats are averaged within circuit before inference; they never increase circuit N. The exact contrast protocol hash is `1484e297156df4e1dedd8d1b9d6dcaaeb0289923e6e9c524fc237609117f2092`.

Architecture inspection confirms that each neural encoder yields **63 × 128 token coordinates**, while its pooled vector has **128 coordinates**. Reconstruction uses the token sequence; prediction/classification uses the mean-pooled vector. The forecast pooling sensitivity additionally evaluates the full 8,064-coordinate token sequence and a fixed 512-coordinate token readout, with the same final d8/d32 and B32/B128 budgets. Small CPU-versus-original-GPU recomputation differences are recorded, and the original saved pooled vectors remain unchanged. These distinct objects must not all be described as a 128-dimensional bottleneck.

The evidence includes train-only quantile and robust-IQR range alternatives, unquantized/projection/quantization attribution, clipping counts, packed payloads, separate shared model/readout storage, logits, class order and confusion matrices. More bits under identical bounds do not correct clipping; the historical masked d8 coordinate clipping of 21.875% remains a limitation. The original 75% rhythm accuracy can be majority guessing, with 50% balanced accuracy, so the new tables always include balanced accuracy. Historical decoder-only cross-validation retains that label. The new frozen-upstream diagnostic uses disjoint train/development/evaluation circuits but does not claim end-to-end cross-validation; all evaluation circuits were previously inspected. H5/H6 superiority claims remain deferred.

## H4: Target and observation scale

Across acquisition variants, the physical short-target state variance is `1.1801433254e-19 V²`; its state-floor/baseline ratio is 0.8121329402. The corresponding long-target ratio remains 0.9588158637. Thus the earlier, shorter target has more estimated state-level improvement available relative to its own baseline, while the tested EEG predictors capture little of it. Raw standardized MSE differs with target-specific training normalization and must not be compared as a common physical scale.

A0 remains byte-for-byte the original nominal 0.5–30 Hz FIR, with DC amplitude gain about 0.928247 and an actual low-pass character. A1 is native nonoverlapping 2-ms binning, not ideal anti-aliasing. A2 is a separately qualified causal third-order Butterworth 4–30 Hz SOS bandpass (six poles). Its measured DC gain is zero, worst 6–24 Hz attenuation relative to peak is 0.615614 dB, and minimum attenuation at or above 250 Hz is 58.948600 dB. Unit-step settling to the declared 1e-4 tolerance takes 872.4375 ms; effective impulse-tail L1 memory at relative tolerance 1e-4 is 862.75 ms. These are tolerance diagnostics for an infinite-memory IIR, not finite support. Its frequency-dependent phase/delay and full-prefix startup are recorded. None backdates sample availability. Physical uncentered DC fractions of approximately 90.67% native/99.05% filtered are not fractions of standardized forecast error. Log mean-square remains explicitly offset-sensitive, distinct from centered AC variance or band power.

T3 cannot be recovered from the old 138-ms future recordings. It is implemented as a future-only one-second orthonormal Fourier block projection with 54 real coordinates, discarding DC and frequencies outside 4–30 Hz. It is available after that whole future block, not as a causal 10-ms-ahead sample. The new target is a prospective scientific choice whose utility is unproven; the original waveform anchors remain in the frozen design.

## Geometry and innovation diagnostics

The geometry analysis completed on **336 unique saved contexts**, deduplicating fork-identical prefixes. It saves physical and train-standardized EEG/representation covariance spectra, participation ratios, variance-threshold PCA counts, pooled and within-circuit/regime summaries, sample-size sensitivity, and privileged physical-state snapshot diagnostics. The input has 2,000 coordinates per context; the original future has 64 coordinates, with 16 scoring futures for each of 16 evaluation contexts. A sample covariance from K contexts has rank at most K−1, and within-context covariance from M futures has rank at most M−1. Adjacent EEG samples and repeated futures are not independent circuit replicates.

The native runtime inventory confirms 40 cells (32E/8I), 880 compartments (40 HH somata plus 840 passive apical segments), 2,560 externally driven contacts and 2,560 active private event generators in this original no-common-event panel. Mean afferent rates are 25 Hz on E contacts and 33.333 Hz on I contacts; shared rate phase is distinct from shared events, and recurrent output spikes are not assumed Poisson. The first audited context has 398 recurrent contacts, 2,958 total Exp2Syn instances and 7,796 stored state coordinates. Its contacts collapse to 900 groups sharing compartment, reversal and kinetics, showing a possible 4,116-coordinate redundancy among stored A/B conductance states. Those contact totals are context-specific, not a universal intrinsic dimension. The circuit operates at canonical 6.3 C and dt = 0.0625 ms; one net three-component dipole is mapped to one scalp channel. These remain toy-model choices.

Stored state layouts vary across circuits. Full stored-state covariance is therefore reported within circuit; an aligned same-location/kinetics/reversal aggregation supplies a separate pooled comparison. Its pooled train-standardized participation ratio is approximately one, but its covariance trace is approximately `3.87e68`: rare synapse coordinates almost constant in training become dominant after standardization on other circuits. This result exposes severe scaling sensitivity and is **not evidence of a one-dimensional biological manifold**. Physical-coordinate companion results are retained. Known circle, planar, noisy-circle and discrete-Poisson controls make the distinction between covariance geometry and intrinsic dimension explicit; nonlinear intrinsic-dimension estimation remains unavailable/unqualified. No single universal dimension estimate is asserted.

The innovation diagnostic splits each evaluation context's 16 futures into two disjoint halves of eight. Across the 16 contexts, their mean cross covariance estimates mean-response geometry; its unsymmetrized sample rank ceiling is 15. The saved estimate is nonsymmetric and its symmetric part is indefinite, with negative eigenvalues retained rather than PSD-clipped. The estimated mean-response trace is `2.551744e-19 V²`, versus mean within-context variance trace `8.802007e-18 V²`; these are sums over 64 coordinates, not per-coordinate risks. The finite-sample decomposition does not close exactly, and this noisy small-context estimate cannot identify exact signal rank. Mean-response geometry also omits task information carried through conditional variance. Full per-neuron voltage/current trajectories are absent from the archive; only stored snapshots are analyzed, and privileged states never enter EEG learners.

## Next decision

The evidence supports a bounded prospective evaluation with transparent acquisition/target contracts and conservative learners. It does not support scaling model size or claiming that a particular acquisition rescues prediction. Freeze the declared protocol, then measure the full-network eight-episode timing gate on the actual workstation before requesting generation. The proposed 832 episodes span eight training, four development and eight evaluation circuits, with eight scoring futures per evaluation context and 32 additional disjoint reference futures for eight prespecified contexts.

The unmeasured generation estimate is **23.416 summed hours** and approximately **4.75 GB native storage**, excluding fitting, derivation, exports and optional instrumentation. It is not execution authorization. Eight evaluation circuits are bounded prospective evidence, not a power guarantee. A later confirmation requires a practical effect/precision target, circuit heterogeneity and multiplicity control. No expensive new circuit generation, old expanded-reference plan, or old confirmation plan was launched during this reanalysis.
